------------------------------- Authentication using Cookies ----------------------------------------

----------- What are cookies ------------

Cookies in web development are small pieces of data sent from a website and stored on the user's computer by the 
user's web browser while the user is browsing. They are designed to be reliable mechanism for websites to remember 
things (very similare to localStorage).

1. Session Management - Cookies allow websites to identify users and track thier individual session states across 
multiple pages or visits. 
2. Personalization - Websites uses cookies for personalized content and ads. 
3. Tracking - Cookies can track users across websites. used for ananytics purpose, advistising targetting and 
websites functionality. 
4. Security : Secure cookies can be used to enhance the security of website ensuring that the transmission of 
information is only done over encrypted connection. 

We'll be focusing on point 4. i.e Security.


--------- Why not localStorage? ---------------------

Cookies and localStorage both provides way to store data on the client-side, but they serve different purposes and 
have different characteristics. 

1. Cookies are send with every request to the website (by the browser)(you don't have to explicitly add a header to
the fetch call).
This point becomes super imp for Next.js , we'll see it soon.

2. Cookies can have expiry attached to them
3. Cookies can be restricted to only https and to certain domains.


-------- Properties of cookies -----------------

Types of cookies:
1. Persistent : Stay even if you close the window.
2. Session : Go away when you close the window.
3. Secure : Sent only over secure, encrypted connections (HTTPS).


Properties of cookies:
-> HttpOnly - Can not be accessed by client side scripts.
-> SameSite - Ensures cookies are not send on cross origin requests.
    1. Strict.
    2. Lax - Only GET requests and on "top level navigation"
    3. None
-> Domains - You can also specify what all domains should the cookie be sent from. 



--------- CSRF Attacks ---------------------

Cross site request forgery attacks were super common because of cookies hence the SameSite Attribute was introduced.

Attack in detail:
    CSRF attacks occur when a malicious website tricks a user into performing an unwanted action on a different 
    website where they are authenticated (e.g., making a purchase, changing account settings, etc.).

    How CSRF works:

    1. The attacker creates a malicious website that includes a hidden request (like a form submission or AJAX 
    request) targeting a site where the victim is logged in.
    2. The victim visits the malicious website.
    3. Since the victim is already authenticated (via cookies or session), their browser sends the request to the 
    target site as if it were legitimate.
    4. The target site processes the request, and the victim's action is executed without their knowledge.


Let's see a few cases of SameSite (i.e solution for CSRF attacks.):

1.-------- None :

App.com ----> cookies goes ---> api.com
malacious.com ---> cookies goes ----> api.com  (which shouldnt be done)

2.-------- Strict:

App.com ----> cookies goes ---> api.com
malacious.com ---> cookies does not go ----> api.com 

but, 

App.com ----> cookies goes ---> api.com
malacious.com--> has button to redirect App.com ---> still cookies does not go ----> api.com (which should go.)

so to solve this, we got Lax.

3.--------Lax , it is the default case as well.
Only GET requests and on "top level navigation"

App.com ----> cookies goes ---> api.com
malacious.com--> has button to redirect App.com ---> cookies goes ----> api.com
malacious.com ---> cookies does not go ----> api.com
